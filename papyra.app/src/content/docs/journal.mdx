The journal is a part of Papyra, next to Notes and To Do. One page for each day,
kept as an ordinary Markdown file in your own folder. It is private: it is never
shared, never read by the assistant, never sent to a webhook, and an `@name` in a
journal page stays plain text.

## Where the files are

Each day is one file, in a folder for its year and month:

```
/data/users/<you>/notes/journal/2026/10/2026-10-25.md
```

A day is only a file once it has something in it. Opening a blank page makes
nothing, and a page you only open and leave never counts as a day. The front matter
carries `kind: journal`, and `mood:` (1 to 5) when you chose one. Everything else is the
text you wrote.

## Writing a day

- **A new day begins at midnight** in your time zone, or later if you ask:
  *Settings → Journal → A new day starts at* goes up to 5 a.m., so something written
  at half past one still belongs to the day that felt like today.
- **How the day felt** is five small pictures, Rough to Great, beside the date. The shape
  of each carries the meaning, not only its colour. Once you choose one it folds into a
  small chip with its name; tap the chip to change it.
- **The page fills the window**, empty or not, so there is always room to start; a long
  day simply runs past the bottom and scrolls.
- **The date opens the calendar**, on that day's month.
- **Notes & to-dos** (top right) opens a panel beside the page with to-dos that still have
  something open and recent notes, each a tap from a link; it stays closed until asked for.
- **Focus** takes the day over the whole browser, with only the date and the writing.
  <kbd>Esc</kbd> leaves the text, then leaves Focus.
- **Type `/`** for what a day is made of: the date, a link to another day or a to-do,
  a photo, a voice note, an embedded to-do list, and the five moods.
- **`[[`** links to a note, a to-do or another day. Say a day the way you would out
  loud: `yesterday`, `last fri`, `sep 14`, `3 days ago`, `2024-03-02`.
- **A to-do inside the day.** *Embed a to-do* shows its checklist in the page. Ticking a
  box there ticks the real to-do. The day's file only holds a reference, so nothing is
  copied and nothing drifts.
- **Carry over** brings the unticked items of the latest earlier day into this one.

## Calendar, streak and "on this day"

*Journal → Calendar* is a month laid out like an almanac page: a wash for each day's
mood, a dot for writing without a mood, a small lock for a locked day, a thumbnail
when the day has a picture. A year view sits beside it. A day counts for the streak
when it has words or a mood. *Settings → Journal → Hide the streak* removes the count
and keeps the totals. "On this day" shows the same date in earlier years.

## Photos and voice notes

Drop, paste or pick pictures as you would in a note. A day with two or more pictures
shows a strip of small thumbnails under the page; choosing one scrolls to it.

**Voice note** records in the browser (it asks for the microphone once) and puts the
recording in the page like any attachment. If your Papyra has a speech model set up
(`Whisper:ModelPath`), the recording is sent as a 16 kHz WAV and, a little later, the
words appear under the player as an italic line. Without one, you simply keep the
recording. A single recording stops at ten minutes.

## Locking a day

**Lock** in the page's footer seals the day with the same vault PIN as locked notes.
On disk the text is scrambled; the mood and the size of the day stay readable so the
calendar and the streak keep working. *Settings → Journal → Locking* can lock new days
as they are made, or every day you have already written.

Locked days are listed on the **Vault** page too, in their own *Journal* section beside
locked notes and to-do lists: you see the date (and the mood, if you set one), never the
words. Each section there folds away; Papyra remembers which ones on that device.

This protects the folder, a Git remote and your backups. The server holds the key, so
anyone who can run your Papyra can read what you wrote; it is not a diary that even
you cannot recover.

## Exporting your journal

*Settings → Data & Storage → Journal export* downloads just the journal, two ways:

- **Markdown files** — a zip laid out like your journal folder: `journal/2026/10/2026-10-27.md`, one file per day,
  with the pictures and recordings those days use under `media/`.
- **PDF book** — every day with writing (or a mood) in one small book: a cover, a contents page, then each month on
  a fresh page with its days run on, in Papyra's own typefaces. Headings, lists, ticked and unticked tasks, quotes,
  code and pictures are laid out; `[[links]]` print as their names. What a page can't play is drawn as the
  small card it is in the journal: a voice note as a mini player with its name and length, a video as its still with a
  play button, a map, a web page or a document as a card saying what it is and where it points — each with its
  caption underneath. Latin scripts print from the book's own faces;
  other scripts print only if the server has a font for them, so use the Markdown export when you need every character.

Both are guarded like the full export: unlock your vault (PIN or passkey) and enter a code from your authenticator
app (or the one emailed to you). Locked days are included, readable, because you opened the vault to ask for them; days
in the Trash are not. Your account gets an email saying that a journal export happened.

## Templates and a reminder

*Settings → Journal → Templates* keeps pages a blank day can start from, with
`{{date}}`, `{{weekday}}` and `{{streak}}` filled in. They show as "Start from" chips
on an empty day; one can be the first offered. Starting from one saves nothing until
you add words of your own. Templates are plain `.md` files under
`.papyra/journal-templates/`, outside the notes folder, and they travel in backups.

*Settings → Journal → Reminder* sends one short email at the time you choose, only if
that day is still blank, and never says anything about what the journal holds. It is
off until you turn it on and needs email to be set up.

## Bringing a journal in

*Settings → Data & Storage → Import* has two journal options:

- **Day One (.zip):** the JSON export, with its photos and recordings. Each entry lands
  on the day it was written, in the time zone it was written in.
- **Daily notes (.zip):** any zip with files named for their day, such as
  `2024-03-02.md`, in any folders. This is how an Obsidian daily-notes folder looks.

A day that already has writing keeps it: what comes in is added underneath, under
"Imported from …". Importing the same file again adds nothing. Locked days, days in
the Trash and days that have not begun are left alone, and the summary says how many.

## Scripts and agents

Make a key with scope **The journal only** (*Settings → API Keys*); its token starts
with `papyra_j_`. It reaches `/api/journal` and nothing else:

```bash
# What's today's page?
curl -H "X-API-Key: $TOKEN" https://papyra.example.com/api/journal/today

# Add a line to today, without reading it first
curl -X POST -H "X-API-Key: $TOKEN" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"text":"Shipped the release."}' \
  https://papyra.example.com/api/journal/2026-10-25/append
```

A key can read and write days, but never archive, lock or delete one and never change
settings. It sees a locked day's date, mood and size, never its words, and cannot write to
one. A key for notes cannot touch the journal at all. Writes are credited to the key in the
day's history.

## Backups and export

Backups and the zip export carry `journal/` along with everything else, and the templates and
journal settings come with a backup. In a backup, locked days travel sealed under
`vault/journal/` and need your password to restore; in the zip export, which you confirm
with your vault open, they come out readable in the same folder. See
[Backups and git](/docs/backups/).
